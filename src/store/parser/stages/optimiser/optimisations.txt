* Predicate pushdown
  - Push down filter expressions as close to the data source as possible (below joins).

  - Some expressions may be able to be pushed down both sides of a join if the filter
    expression can be equated with join predicate expression, and the join is an inner join.
    (note: pushing down a predicate to the right table of an outer join will likely cause
    incorrect results to be generated).

  - A HAVING clause might have an expression on a non-aggregated grouped column that
    can be pushed down to the WHERE clause.
    e.g. select col1, sum(col2) from t group by col1 having col1 > 5;

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
* Projected expression simplification
  - If a projected expression is also a subset of any filter expression then it would
    make sense to calculate that expression once before the filterOp. Otherwise the
    expression would be calculated twice - at the filterOp and in the projectOp.

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
* Join topology
  - Co-partitioned > Partitionable > Broadcast
  - Determine a join order that minimises broadcasting
  - If broadcasting is unavoidable then try to do that join first, as joins tend
    to amplify the number of rows output. If a broadcast join is done later then
    it will likely need to broadcast many more rows

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
* Join algorithm
  - Use table indexes where possible to optimise the join algorithm.
  - Merge Join > Loop/Hash Join

* If there are no joins, can we use indexes and filter expressions to do a range scan
  instead of a full table scan? i.e. push a filter expression into a table scan to turn
  it into a range scan.

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
* Expression simplification
  - Evaluate any literal expressions in the statement.
  - Simplify any nested aggregates in the statement.
      e.g. a SUM(agg(x)) is the same as agg(x)
           a COUNT(agg(x)) is always 1
           a MIN(agg(x)) is always agg(x)
           a MAX(agg(x)) is always agg(x)
           a AVG(agg(x)) is always agg(x)

* An OrderOp (and LimitOp if one exists) can be pushed down through a join if:
  - there are no aggregates in the query
  - the order predicate matches the join predicate
  - the join predicate is an equi-join
  - the join is an inner join (cannot be an outer join)

* Column pruning
  - Instead of reading whole rows from a table, only read the columns that are required.
    i.e. push down a projection above the TableScanOp.

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
* Concert MIN(col1) and MAX(col1) to index lookups
  - Only works in non-GroupBy queries
  - Requires an index on col1
  - Can convert MIN(col1) to SELECT col1 FROM t WHERE col1 IS NOT NULL ORDER BY col1 ASC LIMIT 1
  - Can convert MAX(col1) to SELECT col1 FROM t WHERE col1 IS NOT NULL ORDER BY col1 DESC LIMIT 1

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
* Add a TopN / BottomN operator?
  - Combines OrderBy and LimitOps (without offset).
  - Only needs to sort a limited number of rows. Can discard all others, so more memory efficient.
  - If the limit has an offset then this can be turned into a TopN and another Limit.
      e.g. ORDER BY col1 LIMIT 10 OFFSET 15
      becomes TopN(column=col1, count=25) and a LimitOp(count=10, offset=15)
